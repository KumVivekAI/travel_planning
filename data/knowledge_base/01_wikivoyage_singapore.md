## Overview

Singapore is a city-state in Southeast Asia. It is known for its cleanliness, efficient public transport, multicultural food scene, and mix of modern architecture and heritage neighbourhoods. The island is compact, making it easy to explore multiple districts in a single trip.

## Districts and Neighbourhoods

### Marina Bay and Downtown Core
Marina Bay is Singapore's iconic waterfront district. Key attractions include Marina Bay Sands, Gardens by the Bay (Cloud Forest and Flower Dome), Merlion Park, and the ArtScience Museum. The area is ideal for evening walks along the bay and light shows.

### Chinatown
Chinatown offers temples, shophouses, street markets, and hawker centres. Must-see sites include Buddha Tooth Relic Temple, Sri Mariamman Temple, and Chinatown Heritage Centre. Smith Street and Pagoda Street are popular for food and souvenirs.

### Little India
Little India is vibrant with spice shops, textile stores, and Indian cuisine. Visit Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple, Mustafa Centre, and Tekka Centre for local food. Serangoon Road is the main thoroughfare.

### Kampong Glam (Arab Street)
This heritage district features the Sultan Mosque, colourful shophouses, and Middle Eastern and Malay cuisine. Haji Lane is famous for street art, boutiques, and cafes.

### Orchard Road
Singapore's main shopping belt with malls, department stores, and dining options. Suitable for shopping-focused travellers.

### Sentosa
Resort island with beaches, Universal Studios Singapore, S.E.A. Aquarium, Adventure Cove, and cable car rides. Family-friendly with both outdoor and indoor attractions.

## Major Attractions

- **Gardens by the Bay** — Outdoor Supertree Grove and indoor conservatories; excellent for families.
- **Singapore Zoo and Night Safari** — World-renowned open-concept zoo; Night Safari is a unique evening experience.
- **National Gallery Singapore** — Art museum housed in historic City Hall and former Supreme Court buildings.
- **Singapore Botanic Gardens** — UNESCO World Heritage Site; free entry to main gardens; National Orchid Garden has a small fee.
- **Haw Par Villa** — Eclectic outdoor park with Chinese mythology statues.
- **Jewel Changi Airport** — Rain Vortex and indoor gardens; worth visiting even without a flight.

## Transportation

### MRT (Mass Rapid Transit)
The MRT is the fastest way to travel. Fares are distance-based. Tourists can buy EZ-Link or NETS FlashPay cards for buses and trains.

### Buses
Extensive bus network complements the MRT. Same EZ-Link card works on buses.

### Taxis and Ride-Hailing
Taxis and apps like Grab are widely available. Fares increase during peak hours.

### Walking
Many central neighbourhoods (Chinatown, Little India, Kampong Glam) are walkable. Marina Bay area connects well on foot.

## Cultural and Practical Tips

- English is widely spoken; Malay, Mandarin, and Tamil are also official languages.
- Singapore is hot and humid year-round; carry water and use sunscreen.
- Tipping is not customary; service charge may be included at restaurants.
- Chewing gum is restricted; littering fines are strict.
- Dress modestly when visiting temples and mosques.
- Hawker centres offer affordable, authentic local food.

## Food and Local Experiences

- Try chicken rice, laksa, chili crab, satay, and kaya toast.
- Popular hawker centres: Maxwell Food Centre, Lau Pa Sat, Newton Food Centre, Tekka Centre.
- For a cultural experience, visit a kopitiam (traditional coffee shop) and order kopi or teh tarik.

## Sample Three-Day Itinerary

**Day 1 — Heritage and City**
Morning: Chinatown (temples, heritage centre). Lunch at Maxwell Food Centre. Afternoon: Little India and Kampong Glam. Evening: Marina Bay walk and light show.

**Day 2 — Nature and Culture**
Morning: Singapore Botanic Gardens. Afternoon: National Gallery or National Museum. Evening: Clarke Quay or Robertson Quay dining.

**Day 3 — Sentosa or Zoo**
Full day at Sentosa (beach, aquarium, cable car) OR Singapore Zoo and optional Night Safari.
